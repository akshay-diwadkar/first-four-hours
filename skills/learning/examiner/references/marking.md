# Marking

Read this when a student submits an attempt or asks for marking.

## Record the attempt

Mark only against a known paper, in the set already recording it or, once corrected, the last set in its correction chain. For a paper the student brings that no set records yet, such as a published past paper, create a `supplied-paper` set whose source notes identify the paper and where it came from, and save the paper and any marking scheme or answer key only as the student supplies them; never reconstruct an official scheme and present it as official. Unless the student supplies the official scheme or key, research the official marking scheme or answer key for that paper and session; when you can verify it from the awarding body, cite and link it in the source notes as the set's official basis instead of saving a copy. Only when the student supplies neither a scheme nor a key and no official basis is verifiable, solve and audit the paper as [Papers](papers.md) describes, noting any flaw in the paper instead of changing it, and save your own `answer-key.html`, labelled in it and in the source notes as Examiner's unofficial key, before marking.

Save the attempt as `attempt-<N>.html`, the next attempt number in the set: the student's answers as given, with any image they supplied inline. Label a transcription of handwriting as a transcription and flag uncertain readings instead of guessing.

## Mark

Mark every answer as the examination's markers would, against the set's marking scheme, saved or cited, or, without one, its answer key; with more than one, use the official one or a correction set's corrected file. Apply each rule exactly as that basis or the examination's sourced rules state it, only where they state it, and never one taken from another examination or session: marks tied to assessment objectives and their weighting, levels of response with their mark bands and placement within a band, consequential marking, an error or contradiction negating a correct response, and negative marking with that examination's own arithmetic. The sourced rules are those the paper's instructions or the session's rules for candidates, specification, or examination scheme state: research them for a supplied paper that does not state them, and for an approximation apply its labelled assumptions as assumptions. Scoring rules, such as negative marking, flagged-question scoring, and aggregation, come only from the sourced rules; an unofficial basis never adds or overrides them. A question flagged for review but not answered scores as unanswered unless those rules score it otherwise. Never apply a listed rule neither states, even as a convention; label any other convention you rely on as a convention, never as the scheme's rule. Save `marking-<N>.html` beside the attempt with:

- the basis used: which scheme or key, linked; whether it is official; when it is not, whether it is Examiner's own for this paper, Examiner's unofficial key for a supplied paper, or a correction set's corrected file; and the sourced rules applied, cited;
- for each question, the marks awarded out of those available, any negative mark applied, the assessment objective each mark serves where the scheme ties marks to them, and, for every mark withheld or deducted, the scheme point, level descriptor, or key point missed, or the error or contradiction that negated it, and what the answer needed;
- where the scheme leaves judgment to the marker, such as levels of response, the level and its mark band, the mark placed within the band, why, and the plausible range;
- the total and how it was aggregated, such as a raw sum or each component scaled to its weighting, the percentage of that total, and a grade only when the set is the official paper whose session's published boundaries you apply, labelled as those boundaries applied to this attempt and never as a predicted result;
- the time used in each part, when the attempt records it, as a diagnostic and never part of the score;
- **Diagnosis:** gaps the attempt supports, separating slips from misunderstandings and recurring patterns from single errors, each citing the questions that show it; what one answer cannot establish stays a question to check, not a finding;
- **Next:** targeted follow-up, such as a retry, a focused practice set, or named concepts the student could study with Tutor.

## Retry

A retry of the same paper is its next attempt, recorded as above; follow-up practice is a new set. Re-mark an attempt when a correction changes its key or scheme or a marking error or misreading is found: save `marking-<N>-<M>.html` beside it, `M` from 2, linking the basis applied and keeping the earlier marking, and save a corrected transcription as `attempt-<N>-<M>.html`. When the subject's study plan has an examination section, update it from each attempt's latest marking, never counting a revision as a new attempt.
