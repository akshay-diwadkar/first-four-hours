# Adversarial challenge

One separate fresh-context subagent challenges every draft before handoff; it is never skipped because the change looks small, and it need not be more capable than the planner. Scale its depth to ambiguity and consequence.

## One fresh challenge

Launch the challenger as a separate subagent with fresh context; your own re-read does not replace it. If no separate subagent can be launched, stop without sealing and report that the required challenge could not run. Give the challenger the authoritative request and answers, the draft `blueprint.md`, the relevant source identity, the exact evidence pointers, the [planning contract](planning-contract.md), and this contract. Add a branch reference only when the draft uses its branch: [Declared text](declared-text.md) for Exact, Frozen, or Proof run declarations, [Incomplete gates](incomplete-gates.md) for an environment-sensitive gate, and [Architecture intake](architecture-intake.md) for supplied architecture. Do not give it the README, [Seal receipts](seal-receipts.md), or the planner's reasoning transcript. Brief it with what to read, never with premises to confirm or conclusions to expect.

The challenger is bound by the planning contract's Probe, don't prototype rule: it reads the request, the draft, and source and may run current-tree safe probes, but it writes no file beyond such a probe's own side effects and never applies the draft, writes planned code or tests, or runs anything against a planned end state, in a scratch copy, in memory, or anywhere else; Blueprint's own `check` is not such a run.

Core question:

> Assume a mediocre executor follows this Blueprint literally and uses no useful engineering judgment. How can the result still be wrong, incomplete, unsafe, unnecessarily broad, or unverifiable?

For every material instruction, try to construct a **compliant-but-bad implementation**. Attack at least these dimensions and their examples; the examples are cues to look beyond, not a checklist to stop at:

- **Outcome:** a material decision left open or disguised as executor freedom; a requested outcome, preserved obligation, or affected consumer dropped by the plan's own non-goals; a requirement narrowed or reinterpreted without the user's decision; a compliant result that misses the request; unearned mechanisms such as tamper machinery inside a trusted local environment; inaccurate deliverable Exact text, such as docs or agent-loaded wording.
- **Problem:** wrong current-state, root-cause, owner, or seam assumptions; premises established only below the public entrypoint; repository checks that scan what new code may contain; undecided producible inputs or exit paths, including environment-probing states such as metadata present but unresolvable, unsupported layouts, and reserved names; outputs a consumer cannot act on or audit; completion or resume trusting a recorded status, success marker, or relationship an earlier run established instead of rereading its current source.
- **Scope:** omitted request clauses or imported architecture invariants; missing consumer, contract, or affected Atlas propagation; YAGNI violations; under-scoping disguised as minimality; unearned Exact text or any transcribed implementation.
- **Execution:** hidden choices or materially different faithful implementations; missing preservation; misleading or incomplete snippets; pseudocode settling the wrong branches or order; plan-internal contradictions, such as a later step or check relying on a Source fact an earlier step deletes or rewrites, or an instruction or declared text that makes later proof impossible, like a search check the plan's own Exact text would fail; unsafe ordering, including consuming what a later step creates; missing compatibility, migration, or recovery; completion that requires replanning.
- **Verification:** proof that passes a plausible wrong result or mocks away the claimed operation; a gate that cannot pass on the current tree for a reason outside the planned diff; broad gates (beyond the planned diff) without a recorded current-tree result; a budget claimed to fit without recorded headroom; environment-sensitive gates or `Incomplete:` declarations, signatures, or omissions that break the [Incomplete gates](incomplete-gates.md) rule; unhandled applicable repository traps or foreseeable branches requiring replanning; an owner or human acceptance not marked `Owner gate:`, so Construct would attempt or claim it; executable conditions mislabeled as owner gates, or change obligations hidden there to skip their instruction; a `Run:` that cannot execute verbatim with its executable, accepted arguments, prerequisites, and working directory; selection that misses any part of the planned diff, including renamed and deleted sides; an `Expect:` unjudgeable from its Run's output or unable to tell completion from a plausible failure.

Attack decisions and proof, never plan code's compile, lint, or type correctness. The challenger continues after the first finding through the remaining material scope and reports every independently material gap found in the bounded pass. It advises only: it never rewrites the Blueprint, chooses scope, or owns design. It returns concrete counterexamples and closure obligations, not a claim of thorough review.

Derive every applicable counterexample from source and the authored command path, including unchanged seams; never execute the plan. Re-derive at least the plan's most load-bearing premise yourself from source, and through the authored command's public entrypoint when that premise concerns command behavior; the planner's probe is not that evidence. Trace dispatch when a command crosses a command, adapter, or manifest dispatch seam; inputs, phases, and installed resources when consumed; fixture selection when tests, fixtures, or package surfaces change; interpreter and prerequisites when environment-sensitive; and exit-code contradictions when an Expect admits nonzero exit (the runner passes only exit 0). Support applicable counterexamples with exact source or current-tree probes. When none of these risks applies, no disposition record is earned; do not manufacture work for a simple plan.

For each material finding name the instruction, command, or completion condition; the compliant-but-bad result; decisive evidence or missing fact; and the smallest closure obligation; a missing behavior proof closes only as the planning contract's Step proof allows. Source or a current-tree probe must close current-state claims. An outcome neither can settle follows the planning contract's Probe, don't prototype rule: a non-load-bearing one needs only its prediction label; a load-bearing one needs discriminating authored verification and a decided foreseeable-failure branch. Missing current-state evidence, prediction labels, or required verification and branches remain findings. A restated checklist or an unsupported assertion that a command works is not a finding or closure proof, and an authored Run alone never closes a load-bearing finding. Nothing is closed by executing the plan.

Challenge at least one plausible failure in each materially distinct branch. State untested limits.

## Repair and re-check

Re-verify every finding against the authoritative request and current source, and weigh it by current user authority, evidence, and real consequence; a plan's own non-goal never rejects a finding that names a required outcome. Merge only true duplicate symptoms; accept, reject, or preserve uncertainty from evidence rather than confidence or wording. Supported findings are accepted or rejected against exact source, bounded read-only inspection, or a safe probe as the planning contract defines it. Closure must identify the repaired plan instruction or command and the evidence or fully decided branch that defeats the counterexample; unsupported closure and unresolved load-bearing uncertainty block seal. Keep findings temporary and repair the ordinary draft, not a challenge sidecar. A finding that needs a new fact earns one targeted bounded exploration, never a sweep; a newly material user-owned choice returns to intent closure. When a repair changes direction or a load-bearing premise fails, you own the replacement decision and repair. Locally re-trace the repaired plan for interaction errors among the repairs, and reread each Exact or Frozen block a repair changed as the file now holds it, because an edit can turn an escape sequence into a real line break or break a fence. Then remove or merge work that no longer earns its cost while keeping every decision and instruction the executor needs. Keep a declined finding's reason only when it prevents material rediscovery, as an ordinary Current State fact; never keep a rejection ledger. A second fresh challenge is earned only when repairs replace the plan basis or architecture; any other independent review is earned only by a named material residual risk, never by a security, recovery, or concurrency label alone.

## Requirement trace

Before sealing, trace every clause of the authoritative request, and every imported architecture invariant, constraint, and policy. A change obligation traces to the instruction that realizes it, the Completion Condition that states it, and the Run whose failure would expose its absence, or the named owner instead of that Run when the condition starts `Owner gate:`. A clause that is itself an owner or human acceptance, such as a cold read after implementation, traces only to an `Owner gate:` Completion Condition naming that owner and needs no instruction; any change such a clause also requires is a change obligation with its own instruction. A keep obligation traces to an explicit preservation disposition (a preservation rule in the instruction that touches its surface, or a Completion Condition), plus a discriminating Run only when a plausible compliant implementation could break it. Any other clause with no realizing instruction or preservation disposition is under-scoped; a clause outside an `Owner gate:` that a plausible implementation could violate without failing a Run is unproven; both block seal. Keep the trace in working context, never in the artifact.

## Seal gate

Seal only when all are true:

- user-owned ambiguity is closed;
- decision-critical repository claims are grounded;
- all material engineering and design decisions are settled;
- every request clause and imported invariant traces completely, as the requirement trace defines;
- every producible input state and exit path of changed behavior is decided;
- where completion or a resume relies on earlier recorded state or a relationship that an earlier run of this work established, it is reread at its source, and proof fails an implementation that trusts a stale record or a missing relationship;
- current consumers, preservation, and safety boundaries are accounted for;
- the plan is minimum-sufficient;
- implementation instructions are concrete enough for a mediocre executor;
- every decision takes the planning contract's required form;
- every acceptance condition not marked `Owner gate:` has an executable path;
- supported adversarial findings have evidence-backed closure in the repaired plan; unresolved load-bearing uncertainty blocks seal;
- the separate fresh-context challenge ran;
- no authored implementation step or verification command would force the executor to discover or choose an additional repair, scope change, policy/authority change, ownership change, validation or typing-scope change, exception/budget override, compatibility decision, or other semantic implementation decision;
- verification is discriminating and proportionate.

If substantive judgment is still required anywhere, the Blueprint is not ready to seal. After sealing, stop. A changed request or answer means a fresh draft and workspace; a sealed Blueprint is never edited in place.
